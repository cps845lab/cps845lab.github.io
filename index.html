    In lab (Fri 2:00–3:00)

About this template

Break your product down from the top of the hierarchy to the bottom (Lecture 04, “Example breakdown”):

Vision → Goal / Outcome → Epic → Feature → User Story

How to work with AI

  • Without AI: brainstorm and complete this document yourselves first.
  • With AI: ask AI to do the same work.
  • Decide together: compare the two and decide what goes into this submission: your ideas, AI’s, or a mix.
  • Reflect: complete the AI Reflection and submit it with this document.

What to submit

  • Group: Vision, Goal / Outcome, at least 2 Personas + 3 Epics (at least 1 Feature per Epic) + 3 User Stories per Epic.

Guidance: writing good User Stories

  • Write stories in the customer’s language, without technical detail.
  • Each user story contains one goal of the user: describe what the user wants to do, not how the goal is satisfied.
  • Use personas (a name + profile) to avoid the “super user” syndrome: a user who knows everything and can do anything.
  • Check each story with INVEST: Independent (of other stories) · Negotiable (captures the essence; details negotiated during development) · Valuable (has value for someone) · Estimable (enough detail to estimate the effort) · Small (fits on a card, doable in one sprint) · Testable (so we know when it’s done).

About this template

The Product Backlog is the list of all product features (requirements) that need to be implemented. Each Product Backlog Item specifies the what more than the how.

How to work with AI

  • Without AI: brainstorm and complete this document yourselves first.
  • With AI: ask AI to do the same work.
  • Decide together: compare the two and decide what goes into this submission: your ideas, AI’s, or a mix.
  • Reflect: complete the AI Reflection and submit it with this document.

What to submit

  • Every User Story from your Requirements Breakdown, each with Acceptance Criteria, a Priority and an Estimate.
  • Your team’s Definition of Done.

Guidance: filling in the columns

  • Acceptance Criteria: the conditions of satisfaction for this one item: what must be true for this story to be accepted.
  • Definition of Done: an agreed-upon set of things that must be true before any item is considered complete. It applies to all items; acceptance criteria are specific to one item.
  • Priority: assigned by the customer. Use three levels: Must-have / Good to have but I can wait / Nice to have.
  • Estimate: assigned by the developers, and must include everything needed to get the item to “done”. Your team may choose any one of the scales taught in class. Tick it in Section 1 and use it for every item:

– Story points (relative): 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20

– Effort levels 1–5 = 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 person-days

About this template

The Sprint Backlog is the subset of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, broken down into Sprint Tasks. Critical: Once the Sprint starts, the Sprint Backlog should not change.

How to work with AI

  • Without AI: brainstorm and complete this document yourselves first.
  • With AI: ask AI to do the same work.
  • Decide together: compare the two and decide what goes into this submission: your ideas, AI’s, or a mix.
  • Reflect: complete the AI Reflection and submit it with this document.

What to submit

  • Group:
  • at least 1 User Story selected by the group AND
  • at least 1 Task assigned to each team member

Guidance: Sprint Tasks

  • A task specifies how to achieve the story, and requires one day or less of work.
  • A point person volunteers to be primarily responsible for a task; the task is still owned by the entire team.
  • Estimate each task in hours; re-estimate the remaining effort as work progresses (typically daily).
  • Status follows the task board: To Do → Doing → Done.

About this template

The Sprint Retrospective is the development team reflecting on the Sprint just finished: what went well, what did not go well, and what could be improved for the next Sprint. It is about looking for ways to improve the process.

In this lab, AI is part of your team.

  • Group: your team is your teammates and AI.
  • Individual: your team is you and AI.

What to submit

  • Everyone: Sections 1–3.
  • Section 2: at least 1 entry for “What went well” and at least 1 entry for “What did not go well”. Each entry at least 30 words, with a specific example from this Sprint.
  • Section 3: at least 1 action.
  • Group: answer Sections 2 and 3 together, as one group. You need 1 entry per row from the whole group, not 1 entry per person. For example, a group of 4 writes 1 “What went well” entry together, not 4 separate entries.
  • Group only: Section 4, with 1 row per team member and one specific sentence in each cell. This is the only section where each person answers separately.
  • Individual: delete Section 4. In Section 1, write the User Story you built and list your tasks (individuals do not submit a Sprint Backlog).

Guidance: things to think about (you do not need to answer all of these)

  • AI as a teammate (group and individual): Which tasks did you give AI? Where did it speed you up or slow you down? How did you check its work? Would you give it the same tasks next Sprint?
  • Working as a team (group only): dividing tasks, “Daily Scrums”, merging code, blockers, communication.
